# What Is a Land Witness?

Not everyone has neighbors who can vouch for them.

Someone might have just arrived in an area. Someone might live somewhere too remote for anyone nearby to confirm their claim. And sometimes the only neighbor who could vouch for someone is the very person disputing their land — a more powerful actor who simply won't attest for them, because attesting would work against their own interest.

Landos is built around the person with the least power (Section 2, Pillar 1). A system that only works when you already have people willing to speak up for you fails exactly the people it exists to protect. That's the problem a Land Witness solves.

---

## What a Land Witness Actually Is

A Land Witness is a neutral third party — someone with no relationship to either side of a claim and no stake in the outcome — who witnesses and certifies that a claim or transaction happened the way it says it did. It's the closest thing Landos has to a notary, except the certification is recorded permanently on-chain instead of stamped on a piece of paper.

A Land Witness cannot be a beneficiary of the claim they're certifying. That's not a guideline — it's a structural rule. This is what makes them different from a neighbor (who knows the landowner personally) and different from a validator (who just processes transactions without judging their truth).

---

## When a Land Witness Steps In

Every claim on Landos eventually gets neutral certification as part of the normal registration process. But Land Witnesses matter most in one specific situation: when Neighbor Quorum — the usual requirement that a small group of neighbors confirm your claim — cannot be formed at all.

That happens when no neighbors exist to ask, when the ones who do exist aren't reachable, or when the only relevant neighbor is the person you're in conflict with. In any of those cases, Land Witness review can substitute for Neighbor Quorum entirely.

Because this removes one of the independent signals the system normally relies on, the substitution asks for more, not less: **two independent Land Witnesses**, rather than the usual one, plus a longer minimum period of proven presence on the land. Losing your neighbors' corroboration has to be made up for somewhere, and this is where.

---

## Why This Isn't a Backdoor

Skipping Neighbor Quorum doesn't mean skipping scrutiny. It removes the requirement to proactively gather your neighbors' support — it does not remove anyone else's ability to object.

The Notice Period that comes with every claim stays visible to the whole community hub regardless of which path a claim took to get there. A real neighbor who was never asked to attest can still raise a Contest if something's wrong. Land Witness review makes a claim easier to start. It does not make a claim harder to challenge.

---

## Who Holds Land Witnesses Accountable

Land Witnesses build a reputation — a Personal Validation Score — through their witnessing activity over time. If a Land Witness certifies something fraudulent, that reputation is damaged and they face protocol-level penalties. Being neutral doesn't mean being unaccountable. It means the accountability runs through honesty rather than relationship.

---

## What This Means for the Farmer With No Neighbors

He moved to the area two years ago. The land next to his has sat empty for a decade — no one to ask, no one to confirm what he already knows to be true about the boundary he's worked within. If Landos required a Neighbor Quorum with no alternative, he'd be stuck: correctly occupying land he has no way to formally claim.

Instead, two Land Witnesses — people with nothing to gain or lose from his claim either way — review his case directly. Combined with the extra time he's already spent living and working that land, that's enough to move his claim forward. If a real neighbor ever does turn up with a legitimate objection, the door to contest it is still wide open. He isn't asked to prove more than he can. He also isn't given a shortcut past the truth.

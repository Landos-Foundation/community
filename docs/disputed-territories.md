# What Happens to Land in a War Zone?

Some land sits inside a region that's actively contested — by armed conflict, occupation, or a dispute over which government controls it — at the exact moment people are still living on it and working it.

Landos doesn't wait for that political question to get settled before land there can be registered. But it also refuses to let that same instability become an opening for land theft. Conflict is exactly when the powerful are most likely to grab land from people who have no way to defend a claim in the middle of it, and this is where the protocol pushes back directly.

---

## Conflict Zone Designation

Governance — never Brock alone, never any single actor — can designate a geographic area a Conflict Zone. It's a protocol-level flag on the region, not a ruling on who's right in the underlying conflict. Landos takes no position on that question. It only controls what happens to land claims inside the flagged area while the designation is active.

---

## What Changes Inside a Conflict Zone

Three things, while the designation holds:

- **Claims that were already Confirmed before the conflict are frozen and protected.** They can't be contested, forcibly transferred, or reassigned. Someone can't lose land they already held clean title to just because the region around them became contested.
- **Legitimate owners can still act.** They can file new claims or updates, but these sit in a **Protected Pending** state instead of moving through the normal 28-day staged entry process. The claim is on record. It just doesn't finalize while the zone is active.
- **New claims with no history before the conflict are quarantined.** They're visible on-chain — nothing is hidden or erased — but blocked from advancing toward Confirmed until the designation changes. Anyone can see a claim was filed. No one can ride a conflict into a clean deed.

---

## Getting Back to Normal Isn't Automatic

A Conflict Zone doesn't just snap back to ordinary rules the moment fighting stops. It moves through four stages, and each one requires a fresh governance approval to advance — there's no calendar timer quietly counting down in the background:

1. **Active Conflict** — the zone as originally designated. Everything above applies.
2. **Post-Conflict Review** — opens once governance judges that conditions on the ground have actually changed. Frozen and quarantined claims get reviewed here. Nothing is released yet.
3. **Pending Resolution** — claims found legitimate during review start moving again, but under a time lock before anything is finalized.
4. **Restored** — the zone returns to normal status-spectrum rules. No more special handling.

Each stage is a deliberate checkpoint, not a countdown. Conditions have to be judged genuinely changed before the next stage opens.

---

## Why It's Built This Way

Freezing what's already Confirmed and quarantining anything new removes the incentive to treat conflict as a land grab. There's nothing to gain by pushing someone off land they already held clean title to mid-conflict, and nothing to gain by rushing in behind them to register it as your own — the claim just sits quarantined, going nowhere, until the region stabilizes and governance says so.

This is the same founding principle running through the whole protocol, applied to its hardest case: **land acquired by force is not recognized**, no matter what paperwork gets produced afterward, and no matter how long the fighting lasts.

---

## What This Means for the Family Still on Their Land

They registered their land years before the fighting started in their region. Confirmed status, clean history. When the conflict zone designation goes into effect, nothing changes for them — their claim is frozen exactly as it stood, untouchable by anyone trying to exploit the chaos to take it.

Meanwhile, someone who shows up during the conflict and files a claim over land with no prior record gets nothing but a quarantined entry — visible, but stuck, until governance reviews the whole region once things calm down. The family doesn't have to fight to defend what's already theirs. The protocol is already defending it, just by refusing to let anything move.

# Trying Out a Land Claim (For Now)

This explains, in plain language, how to file and process a practice land claim before the real Landos network exists. If you're technical, the short version is in [README.md](README.md). This version assumes no technical background.

## The short version

There's no real Landos blockchain yet, so there's nowhere real to register land. But the rules for how a claim would move from "someone says this is theirs" to "confirmed, hard to dispute" are already written down. This is a way to actually practice those rules using GitHub — the website Landos's documents live on — instead of waiting for the real thing to exist.

**Important: this is not a real land registry.** Nothing filed here is a legal claim to anything. Use a made-up or clearly fake location for your test parcel — never a real address or a real piece of land, yours or anyone else's.

## What you need

A free GitHub account (create one at github.com if you don't have one) and a spot on the [participants list](PARTICIPANTS.md) — ask in the [Discord](https://discord.gg/DBNpwm7MjW) or reach out to Brock directly to be added. This list is separate from the governance members list — attesting to or witnessing a test claim isn't a governance vote.

## Step by step

**1. File a practice claim.**
Post a short description of a made-up parcel — what it is, roughly where it's supposed to be (fictional or clearly labeled as a placeholder) — following the [claim template](templates/PARCEL_CLAIM.md). This is your claim's starting point.

**2. A week of visibility.**
Your claim sits out in the open for a week. Anyone can raise a concern about it. No one votes yet — this week is just so nothing moves forward unnoticed.

**3. Other people vouch for you — or two Land Witnesses do instead.**
Normally, three other participants need to vouch that your claim looks legitimate. If there aren't enough people around to do that yet (likely, while the group is still small), two neutral participants can review and certify it directly instead — this is the same "no neighbors available" fallback the real design provides for people with no one nearby to ask.

**4. You check in a few times over a week.**
The real system would track this with a phone's GPS over time. Here, you just post two or three short updates describing how you're engaging with your test claim. It's a stand-in, not the real thing — everyone understands that.

**5. Certification.**
The same neutral reviewer(s) from step 3 sign off, and your claim's status updates to Confirmed.

**6. If someone disagrees.**
Anyone can raise an objection at any point, and the claim's status changes to Contested until the group works it out together.

## Why bother, if it's not real?

Two reasons. It gives you something to actually do while the real network doesn't exist yet. And it's the best way for the group to find out, cheaply, whether the actual rules Landos is being built on hold up when real people try to follow them — before any of it becomes code that's hard to change.
